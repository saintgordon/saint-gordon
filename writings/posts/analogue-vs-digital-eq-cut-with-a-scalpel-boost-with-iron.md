Every EQ move falls into one of two jobs: clearing garbage out of the way, or adding size and shine that wasn’t there before. Picking the wrong tool for the job makes mixes feel sterile or muddy fast.

> Cut unwanted frequencies with clean digital precision. Boost tone and weight with broad analogue colour.

## Digital EQ: Your Surgical Scalpel

Fully digital EQs (`FabFilter Pro Q`, `TDR Nova`, stock DAW parametric EQs) add zero harmonic colouration or transformer grit. They let you pick exact frequencies, dial razor tight Q widths, and trigger dynamic bands only when a problem frequency jumps out.

Reach for digital EQ when cleaning up:

* **Non Musical Rumble:** Drop a high pass filter (`12` or `18 dB/octave` standard, `6` gentler, `24+` steeper) to strip useless sub air below instruments that don’t belong there.
* **Muddiness (`200 to 400 Hz`):** Narrow to medium Q dip on guitars, piano, and thick bass.
* **Boxiness (`400 to 600 Hz`):** Medium Q dip on kick, toms, and acoustic body.
* **Vocal Nasality (`1 to 2 kHz`):** Tight Q cut whenever a vocal sounds honky or boxy.

Watch out for mixing with your eyes on a spectrum graph: stacking ten tiny surgical notches usually hollows out life and phase faster than leaving a slightly imperfect track alone.

## Analogue EQ: Broad Curves & Harmonic Iron

Analogue hardware emulations (`Pultec EQP1A`, `Neve 1073`, `API 550A`, `SSL 4k`) give you wide Q bandwidths, fixed frequency steps, and subtle saturation from virtual tubes and transformers. Fewer knobs force faster mixing by ear.

Reach for analogue EQ when adding character:

* **Pultec Low Weight (`100 Hz`):** Boost and attenuate slightly together on bass or vocals for thick low end that never turns flabby.
* **Neve or SSL Mid Bite (`1 to 2 kHz`):** Small boost to help clean guitars, distorted guitars, or bass guitar attack cut right through dense drums.
* **Air & Shine (`4 to 5 kHz` and `12 kHz`):** Gentle `1073` vocal presence around `4 to 5 kHz` or wide `12 kHz` Pultec shelf for glossy open air.

## The Three Step EQ Workflow

Follow this order on any crowded mix before reaching for another fader:

1. **Cut First (Digital, Narrower Q):** Remove rumble, ringing resonances, and mud cleanly.
2. **Boost Second (Analogue, Wider Q):** Add back warmth, attack, or open top end musically.
3. **Clear Masking Third:** When guitars bury the vocal, stop cranking the vocal fader louder. Check where the vocal shines (often `4 kHz`), then place a small, tight bell dip at `4 kHz` on the guitars so both sit in their own pocket cleanly.
